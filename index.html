01

The Non-Negotiables

Coaching note

These are not goals. They are operating parameters. A pilot doesn't decide whether to run the pre-flight checklist based on how he feels. You don't decide whether to do these based on mood, stress, or how late you stayed up. The word "non-negotiable" means exactly that — it is not up for discussion. Especially with yourself.

📖 Bible + Prayer — before all inputs

Scripture, then prayer — before email, phone, news, or social. Set the anchor in truth and gratitude before the world sets it for you. Focus on thankfulness, wisdom, leadership, and stewardship. Minimum: one chapter + three minutes of focused prayer. Non-compressible.

🛏 Make the bed — immediately

The moment your feet hit the floor. Not after coffee. Not after the bathroom. Your first act of discipline is to bring order to your space. This is your first win of the day. Take it.

💧 Hydrate — 16 oz minimum

Your brain is dehydrated after sleep. Stage it the night before — zero friction, zero decision. This is not optional wellness advice. It is basic maintenance on the most important machine you own.

📰 Read — business books + news

15 minutes: one chapter of a business book plus a scan of Axios, Reuters, or WSJ. Leaders who stop learning stop growing. Know your industry. Know the market. Know what's moving before your first call.

👔 Get fully dressed and ready

Not gym clothes. Not loungewear. Dressed and ready as if you have a meeting with someone you respect. Identity is enacted, not felt first. Your posture and confidence follow your appearance.

📞 T-Town Pristine Clean — daily outreach

10 calls and outreach emails every single day. Commercial cleaning is a volume game. Pipeline dries up when outreach stops. This is a non-negotiable revenue activity — not a to-do item. It happens before you get pulled into anything else.

⏰ Work begins at a defined time

Pick your start time and protect it. When that time arrives you are in the chair, on the call, or taking the action. The transition from morning routine to work is a hard line — not a gradual drift.

Hard truth

Every time you break a non-negotiable, you teach your brain that your commitments are negotiable. That compounds. In six months you won't just have a bad morning habit — you'll have a character habit. The stakes are not productivity. The stakes are integrity with yourself.

The 10X Activity Planning Session

Cardone principle

"Success is your duty, obligation, and responsibility." The 10X Rule is not about working more hours. It's about thinking at a different level of action. Most people set minimum targets and hit minimums. You're going to set massive targets and compress time.

List itemWhat to writeWhy it matters
10People to contact today Names only. Sales prospects, partners, clients, referral sources, T-Town leads, people you've been avoiding. Forces thinking in terms of relationships and pipeline, not tasks. Your network is your net worth.
10T-Town outreach targets 10 specific commercial properties, property managers, or facility contacts. Name + number + one-line pitch. These get called before noon. Commercial cleaning is a pipeline business. Outreach volume is the only variable you fully control. 10 a day = 200+ touches per month.
5Calls you least want to make The follow-up you owe. The uncomfortable ask. The confrontation you've delayed. The prospect who might say no. Procrastination lives here. These 5 calls represent most of your stalled progress. Do the hard calls first.
3Highest-leverage activities What actually moves your business trajectory today? Not what's on your list — what changes the outcome? The $10 tasks crowd out the $10,000 tasks. This forces you to distinguish between busy and productive.
1Single win that defines today If you could only accomplish one thing, what would make today a success? Write it at the top. Circle it. Clarity is power. Define the win in advance and you stop confusing motion with progress.
1The 10X move The action most people in your position would avoid — too bold, too direct, too exposed. The one requiring real courage. Massive action is uncomfortable by definition. If it doesn't make you slightly uncomfortable, it's not 10X.
Coaching challenge

Look at your T-Town list right now. Are those real targets with real numbers, or are they vague names you'll avoid calling? A name without a number is a wish. A number without a scheduled call time is procrastination. Write the name, write the number, make the call before noon. Every day.

04

Anti-Procrastination Protocol

These are hard rules, not suggestions. Each one addresses a specific flavor of procrastination.

The "I'll do it later" rule

When you catch yourself saying this, your obligation doubles. Do it immediately, or schedule it for a specific time in the next two hours and set an alarm. "Later" without a timestamp does not exist.

Reframe → "Later is where good intentions go to die. I do it now or I schedule it with a hard commitment."

The scrolling / avoidance rule

Scrolling, organizing your desktop, checking email a third time, or any low-value activity while a high-value task sits undone — that is not a break. That is procrastination wearing a costume. Name it out loud. Return to the task.

Reframe → "This is avoidance. I am not resting — I am hiding. Hiding is not a strategy."

The over-planning rule

Preparation that never ends is procrastination with a PhD. You don't need another framework or outline. You need to make the call. Research and planning have a return on investment only when they directly enable action. When they replace action, they're obstacles.

Reframe → "Clarity comes from engagement, not preparation. I will know more after I start."

The broken streak rule

You miss a morning. It happens. The rule: never miss twice. One miss is an event. Two misses is a pattern. Three misses is an identity. Do not beat yourself up — diagnose. System gap or discipline gap? Fix it tonight. Execute tomorrow.

Reframe → "Missing once is human. Missing twice is a choice. I choose differently tomorrow."

The motivation fallacy rule

Motivation follows action — it does not precede it. Waiting until you "feel like" working is waiting until you're thirsty to drill a well. You don't need to feel ready. You need to be moving.

Reframe → "I don't need to feel like it. I need to do it. My feelings follow my actions — not the other way around."

The T-Town first-call rule

The first T-Town call happens before 9:30 AM. Not at noon when you "have a second." Not after the meeting. Before 9:30. Decision-makers are available, your energy is high, and the rest of the day's outreach follows naturally from a strong first call. Set the standard in the morning.

Reframe → "The first call is the hardest. After that, momentum carries the rest."

The Identity Shift

James Clear — Atomic Habits

Every action is a vote for the type of person you want to become. You don't rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems. But before systems, there is identity. You can't consistently act against who you believe yourself to be. The work here is to change the belief.

I am someone who procrastinates
I am a disciplined operator
"I take immediate action. I do not negotiate with myself. Discipline is who I am — not something I practice when convenient."
I'll start when I feel motivated
I execute before I feel ready
"I have been built and called for this. I don't wait for readiness — readiness comes through action. I move first."
I struggle to keep commitments to myself
I keep my word to myself above all others
"Self-trust is built by doing what I said I would do, especially when I don't want to. Every kept commitment is compound interest on integrity."
I am managing businesses
I am a high-output business builder and steward
"I am a steward of what God has entrusted to me — including T-Town Pristine Clean. I build with excellence and operate at a level worthy of the assignment."
I work hard when I get started
I am a man of faith who leads from the front
"My faith is not passive. It is expressed in how I lead my family, my teams, and myself. I show up because I am accountable to something eternal."
I know what I should do but delay it
I close the gap between knowing and doing
"The gap between who I am and who I'm meant to be is closed one morning at a time. Today's execution is tomorrow's character."
Daily practice

Read your identity statement aloud during prayer. This is not a motivational exercise — it's a neurological one. Repeated verbal affirmation activates the prefrontal cortex and creates new default patterns. Spoken identity statements change behavior faster than written ones. Use your voice. Mean it.

Routine Tiers — Consistency Without Perfection

Willink principle

Discipline equals freedom. Life will compress your morning. Travel will disrupt your routine. The answer is not to skip — it's to have a tier for every situation. You never miss. You just shift tiers.

Minimum viable — <15 min
  • No snooze. Feet on floor.
  • Drink 16 oz water
  • Read one Bible verse + 60-second prayer
  • Get fully dressed
  • Write today's single most important task
  • Make 3 T-Town calls minimum
  • Begin work
Use when: travel, family emergency, chaos
Standard — 75 min
  • No snooze. Bed made immediately.
  • Hydrate 16 oz
  • Bible + prayer (15 min)
  • Business reading + news (15 min)
  • Movement 15 min
  • Shower + fully dressed
  • 10X planning + T-Town list
  • Work begins on time
Use on: most days, 5+ days per week
Elite — 90+ min
  • Wake earlier than needed
  • Hydrate + light stretch first
  • Deeper Bible study + journaling
  • Extended prayer — intercede for others
  • Full business chapter + in-depth news
  • Full workout — 30–40 min
  • Fully dressed + groomed
  • 10X planning + T-Town list (full 10)
  • Begin with single most important task
Use when: low-travel weeks, peak seasons
The non-compression rule

The one item that never shrinks across any tier is Scripture and prayer. Everything else compresses under pressure. The spiritual foundation does not. Even on the minimum viable day, God comes before the laptop. T-Town outreach is the second non-compressible — at least 3 calls on minimum days.

08

The 90-Day Execution Plan

Days 1–30 — Install

Build the system

  • Set your hard wake time
  • Set work-start time — don't move it
  • Stage environment the night before
  • Start T-Town outreach list daily
  • Use minimum viable if needed — never skip
  • Weekly score target: 65+
  • Do not add complexity yet
Days 31–60 — Strengthen

Deepen the habits

  • Increase to standard routine most days
  • Weekly scoring review every Sunday
  • Identify your #1 procrastination pattern
  • Add one accountability partner
  • Track T-Town call conversion rate
  • Weekly score target: 75+
  • Expand 10X list targets
Days 61–90 — Compound

Raise the standard

  • Standard or elite routine daily
  • Scorecard should feel easy at 80+
  • Review business results: what changed?
  • Review T-Town pipeline growth
  • Set next 90-day revenue targets
  • Weekly score target: 85+
  • You are operating at a new level

Weekly review — every Sunday (15 minutes)

What was my weekly scorecard average? What drove the variance?

Which days did I miss — and what was the actual reason? Be honest.

Did I make all 10 T-Town calls every day, or did I rationalize a shortcut?

Did I complete the 5 hardest calls on my list, or did I avoid them again?

What is the single outcome from this week that demonstrates 10X thinking?

What am I rationalizing as "preparation" that is actually delay? Name it.

Did my faith and prayer life lead my week — or follow it?

What is the one thing I will do differently in the next 7 days?

Final word — coaching challenge

You have everything you need. You know what to do. You have the knowledge, the businesses, and the capability. What you are fighting is not a lack of information — it is the gap between knowing and doing. That gap is closed by one thing: execution at the moment of resistance.

The person you want to become exists on the other side of the morning you're about to have. Don't negotiate your way out of it. Don't optimize your way around it. Just begin — before motivation, before clarity, before readiness.

"You will never be ready. You will only be willing."
